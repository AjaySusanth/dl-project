## 2. Pre-coding briefing

### What I planned to do

Build six-feature input sequences and multi-hour targets for train, validation,
and test. Inputs may look into the past across a split boundary, but every
target must stay inside its assigned split.

### Why this task is needed

Recurrent models do not consume a flat table directly. They need sequences of
past hourly observations and the future values that the sequence should
forecast.

### Expected files

- `src/features/windows.py`
- `docs/reports/M2-T4.md`

## 3. What I implemented

`build_windows` returns one NumPy-array mapping for each split with:

- `X`: `(N, lookback, 6)` feature sequences.
- `zone_idx`: `(N,)` embedding indices.
- `y_scaled`: `(N, horizon)` standardized log-demand targets.
- `y_raw`: `(N, horizon)` raw pickup targets.
- `origin_ts`: `(N,)` last input timestamps.
- `zone_id`: `(N,)` TLC zone IDs.

The six input features follow the architecture order:

1. scaled log pickups;
2. sine and cosine of hour of day;
3. sine and cosine of day of week;
4. weekend indicator.

Targets are selected from the first hour after the origin through the
configured horizon. Samples whose final target exceeds the split end are
discarded. Validation and test windows can use earlier observations as input,
as required by the architecture.

### Explain it simply

Each sample is a short movie of the recent past plus the next few hours the
model must predict. The movie may begin before validation or test, but the
predicted future must belong entirely to that evaluation period.
